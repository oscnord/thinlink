# AGENTS.md: crates/policy

`sat-teleop-policy` (L0) holds pure decisions: the in-picture stamp and the state stamp, the byte-rate budget, the uplink budget, the adaptation ladder and its controller, the sender's pacing admission (`pace`), the control lease, video freshness, the predictive display's prediction and drawing, clock bounds (`clock`, with the rule that ends a hand-measured bound when the wall clock steps), retention of retained snapshots and when retrieval may send. It depends only on `std`, `thiserror`, `serde` (derives only) and `subtle` (the token comparison): no I/O, no clock reads (time arrives as an argument), no GStreamer, MAVLink, HTTP, filesystem or sockets. See the root `AGENTS.md`.

## In-picture stamp (`stamp`)

The stamp puts frame identity and capture time into the video itself, so the lab can measure frame age and new-frame cadence end to end.

- Payload, 9 bytes: `frame_id: u32` big endian, `capture_ms: u32` (UNIX epoch milliseconds modulo 2^32) big endian, then `crc8` over the first 8 bytes: CRC-8/I-432-1 (polynomial 0x07, init 0x00, xorout 0x55), so an all-zero or all-0xFF payload, a uniform stamp area, never validates. 72 bits, MSB first.
- Drawn into the luma plane of I420 frames: 24 columns x 3 rows of square blocks at the top-left, row-major, one bit per block. Block side is `width / 24` (integer division), minimum 8 px. Bit 1 is luma 235, bit 0 is luma 16.
- `draw` rejects frames narrower than 192 px or shorter than 3 blocks, a stride below the width, overflowing geometry and buffers shorter than `stride * height`, all as `StampError`.
- `read` samples the centred half-size square of each block (mean above 128 is 1) and returns `None` on a CRC mismatch or bad geometry.
- `check_size` tells whether a frame size can carry the stamp; `CaptureMs::from_unix_ms` reduces UNIX milliseconds to the stamp's 32-bit clock.
- An 8-bit CRC still passes about 1 in 256 corrupt reads, for example of concealed frames after packet loss. Readers must bound the age they accept: `plausible_age` returns the age only within 0 to `MAX_PLAUSIBLE_AGE_MS` (60 s).
- `age_ms(receiver_ms, stamp)` is `receiver_ms.wrapping_sub(capture_ms)`. It is only valid when both sides share a clock (all testbed containers do); across machines a clock mapping with uncertainty is required (`docs/measurement.md`).

## State stamp (`stamp`)

The ground's freshness output draws what it shows into each frame, so the lab can read the state from the video itself.

- `StateStamp { state, age_10ms }`: 24 bits, MSB first, as one more row of 24 blocks right below the stamp (same block side and luma levels; the stamp stays as it is): 4 bits the state's code (`freshness::State::code`, 1-6), 16 bits the newest frame's age in 10 ms units, clamped (all ones also when unknown), 4 bits CRC-4/INTERLAKEN (polynomial 0x3, initial value and final xor 0xF) over the first 20. Codes 0 and 7-15 are unused, so a uniform black or white row never reads as a state.
- `draw_state` and `read_state` reuse the block grid; they need 4 rows of blocks (`check_state_size`). A CRC-4 lets about 1 in 16 corrupt rows through, and any picture's row below the stamp reads as a state now and then, so a state stamp means something only in the ground output's video.

## Predictive display (`predict`, `mark`)

`predict::aim` and `predict::horizon` turn the autopilot's newest attitude into pixels with the camera's horizontal field of view, and `predict::mark` decides whether to draw at all: only for `Live` or `LowRate` with a known age. They return `None` for an attitude older than `MAX_ATTITUDE_AGE_MS` (1 s), a field of view outside 0 to 180 degrees and non-finite input, and clamp the aim to the picture. `mark::draw` puts a dashed hollow ring and a dashed line into the luma plane, below the stamp rows, which it never touches. Never fill the ring or draw solid lines, and never draw for a picture that is not current: the mark must not pass for part of the scene or make old video look live. Time arrives as arguments (`now_ms`, `lead_ms`).

## Detail inset (`inset`)

`inset::place(state, age_ms, inset_size, picture)` decides where the pilot's detail inset goes and whether it shows: only for `Live` or `LowRate` and a crop under `MAX_AGE_MS` (30 s) old, at the top right below the stamp rows (`stamp::state_rows_px`) with a label row above and a 2 px frame, every coordinate even for the chroma planes, `None` when it does not fit. `fit` scales a crop to at most a third of the picture's width and half its height in its aspect, `widen` grows the box 10 % each side for the request, `label` writes `DETAIL 3.4 s`. A table test pins the show and hide rules and the placement; never draw an inset for a state that is not current.

## Freshness (`freshness`)

`classify(Thresholds, Observed) -> State` decides what the ground's video output shows: `Live`, `LowRate`, `Stale`, `Frozen`, `Disconnected` or `AgeUnknown`. The ground passes in elapsed milliseconds and counts, never a clock.

- In order: `Disconnected` when the source pipeline is down or no frame at all arrived for `disconnected_after_ms`; `AgeUnknown` when fewer than `min_readable_percent` of the recent frames had a readable stamp; `Frozen` when frames keep arriving (one within `frozen_after_ms`, at a low rate plus the frame interval) but none was new for longer than that; `AgeUnknown` without a clock bound; `Stale` when the newest new frame's age plus the clock bound exceeds the stale threshold; `LowRate` when the source's frame interval exceeds `low_rate_interval_ms`; otherwise `Live`. At a low rate the stale threshold is `low_rate_stale_after_ms`: such a picture ages by a whole frame interval between frames, which against `stale_after_ms` would flicker between Live and Stale, and it is never called Live. So only video provably younger than its stale threshold is Live or LowRate, and a blackout (nothing arrives) shows Stale, then Disconnected, never Frozen.
- The frame interval is `cadence_ms` of the new frames' ids and capture times: the lower median of the capture time per frame id between consecutive frames (a pair whose id does not advance is skipped, so unreadable frames between two readable ones do not slow the source) whose later frame was captured within a second of the newest. One gap at full rate (a lost frame) leaves it at the full rate's interval, so a stream that then stops reads Stale at `stale_after_ms`, not at the low-rate threshold; after a slowdown it reads slow within two slow frames.
- `next_change_ms(Thresholds, Observed)` says how soon `classify` may give another state if no frame arrives: the earliest of the newest frame's age plus the clock bound passing the stale threshold, the time since the newest new frame passing the frozen threshold and the time since the last frame passing `disconnected_after_ms` (`None` when none lies ahead). The output repeats its last frame no later than that.
- `LabelDeadline` is the check at the other end: a current frame's state holds until the moment `next_change_ms` gave when it was decided, and a frame whose first packet is sent after that moment is late in all its packets (the output drops it). Times are on one clock, in any unit.
- The thresholds come from `[freshness]` of `testbed/limits.toml`, validated by `sat-teleop-media` (`docs/measurement.md` derives them).

## Byte-rate budget (`rate`)

`TokenBucket` limits a byte rate and keeps headroom for important traffic: it refills at `bytes_per_s` up to one second of budget; `Priority::Preferred` may spend it down to empty, `Priority::Bulk` only down to half. Over budget, bulk is refused first while preferred traffic still fits. `take(now, bytes, priority)` takes time as an `Instant` argument and never reads a clock; time going backwards refills nothing. `set_rate` changes the rate from then on. The vehicle's telemetry budget (`sat-teleop-control`) uses it.

## Uplink budget (`budget`)

`Budget::split(total, usage, retrieval_wanted)` divides an uplink rate, in bytes per second on the wire, in order: control (the larger of the reserve, the probes and challenges at their largest, and what the vehicle measured of its probes and every control reply), telemetry (`telemetry_percent` of the MAVLink frame bytes the autopilot offers, the cap until that is measured, between `telemetry_floor` and `telemetry_cap`, plus the headers of the datagrams the vehicle measured it sending), retrieval (what it asks for, zero while idle) and video (the rest). Each takes at most what is left, so the shares never add up to more than the total, and at a small total video gets nothing. `Budget::telemetry_cap(total, usage)` is the cap for the telemetry bucket in frame bytes: the telemetry share of a measured uplink, the configured cap while there is none, and never below the floor, so the vehicle's state keeps flowing however short the uplink.

- `wire_bytes(payload)`: a datagram on the wire, Ethernet (14), outer IPv4 (20) and UDP (8), `WireGuard`'s data header (16) and tag (16) around the inner IPv4 (20) and UDP (8) headers and the payload, padded to 16 bytes. `DATAGRAM_OVERHEAD` (110) is the same for a datagram of unknown size, its padding taken at the mean (7.5 bytes, rounded up). `video_bytes_per_s` adds RTP's 12-byte header and those headers per packet to a bitrate.

## Adaptation (`adapt`)

A `Ladder` of `Rung`s (size, frame rate, bitrate, `Group`: normal, constrained, awareness only), best first, each sending less than the one above. `Controller::observe(feedback, now_ms) -> Decision` walks it from the relay's answers; the caller also calls it without one at least every probe interval, so silence is noticed. Latency first: it keeps the uplink queue empty and only then raises picture quality. The thresholds are `[adapt]` of `testbed/limits.toml`, validated by `sat-teleop-media` (`docs/measurement.md` derives them).

- Queue delay is the smaller round trip of the newest two answers minus the smallest of the `min_rtt_window_ms` window: one late answer is jitter, two in a row are a queue. Its trend, the least-squares slope over `trend_window_ms` of probes sent since the last change (leaving out the first, whose filter partner came before the change), rises, drains or stands (`trend_ms_per_s`), and a standing one may still fall.
- Above `queue_delay_high_ms`, rising: the uplink carries less than is sent, so the uplink measured with that answer (`Feedback::uplink_bytes_per_s`) is what it carries. Cut to the best rung that fits `capacity_fit_percent` of it after the reserve (`budget`), at least one rung, so the rest drains the queue, and remember that capacity for `capacity_hold_ms` (with nothing measured yet, cut one rung). Standing and not falling at all: cut one rung. Not falling at all after a queue cut: the cut changed nothing, so the path's base delay grew (a route change); take the cut back (`BaseShift`) and count round trips only from then on, at most once per base window. Falling, however slowly, or draining: hold.
- Deep: one answer to a probe sent since the last change more than twice `queue_delay_high_ms` late (jitter never comes close), unless that change was a queue cut. It is cut as rising at once, also within the change interval, since it already shows the last change. Any answer at least `drain_above_ms` late is a `Drain`, with a cut when one is due and otherwise at the rung it has (within the change interval, or while a cut drains the queue slowly), except after a drain that drained nothing until the rung changes or the delay falls under the threshold (the base grew): no video until an answer to a probe sent since shows the queue at the base (`Resume`), or until the queue delay is not draining over the trend window (no queue then: the base grew, and the base-shift rule takes the cut back with the video running). Answers to probes sent before the drain come back through the queue that held them, so they measure the uplink again (the lower measurement counts: at a deep cut the relay's windows may reach back to before the queue; a probe sent during the pause measures nothing, since only the little the vehicle sent meanwhile got through just ahead of it), and the video resumes at the best rung that fits, a `Step` when that is lower. Silence ends a drain too: the floor, with video.
- Loss in GCC's bands (draft-ietf-rmcat-gcc-02, section 6), over `loss_window_ms` and at least `loss_min_packets` video packets since the last change: below `loss_low_percent` a rung up may come, above `loss_high_percent` one rung down, in between hold. Fewer packets say nothing, which is what keeps random loss from pinning the ladder.
- One rung up after `dwell_ms` of healthy answers (queue delay at most `queue_delay_base_ms`, loss low or unknown) to probes sent since the last change, unless the capacity measured cannot carry the rung and that rung's hold has not run out. Each rung's hold starts at `capacity_hold_ms` and doubles, counted from that cut, up to `capacity_hold_max_ms`, whenever a climb to it is cut within a dwell; a later cut that measures at least 10 % more uplink than at the failure resets it (less is within a measurement's noise, about 7 %).
- A climb into a rung that costs more than `PROBE_PERCENT` (110 %) of the uplink measured at the last cut is paced: the rung starts at 110 % of the uplink, and every `min_change_interval_ms` of healthy answers the ceiling goes up by the same factor (`Reason::Probe`, `Controller::ceiling_kbit`; not a rung change) until it reaches the rung's own bitrate. A queue builds for about a round trip before the controller sees it, so it peaks at about the round trip times the excess over the uplink. A cut that follows a climb or a raise within `dwell_ms` fails the rung, whether it measured the uplink or not. The capacity is kept until a later cut measures it again or a base shift clears it, however old: with none (the start, or after a base shift) the climb is not paced.
- No answer for `stale_after_ms`, once one came: the floor at once (before the first answer the start rung stands). Unless the last answer showed a deep queue, the silence is an outage: the rung before it and the capacity are kept, and `outage_recovery_ms` of healthy answers after it bring that rung back (`Recovered`), or the best below it that the capacity carries; like a climb, a jump back cut within a dwell doubles that rung's hold. Otherwise at most one change per `min_change_interval_ms`, a deep queue aside; every signal counts only answers to probes sent since the last change, so nothing changes again before the last change shows in the feedback.
- `Controller::pace_bytes_per_s` is what the sender's pacer holds the video to (`pace::rate`): between the wire cost of the bitrate allowed the current rung (its ceiling while a climb is paced) and 120 % of it, and within that 110 % of the uplink believed (the capacity at the last cut, or the most tested since: `proven`, set by each raise and cleared at a cut). Never below the allowance: a pace under what the encoder is told to send drops frames the relay reads as loss. `pace::Admission` decides which frames the pacer's backlog takes. `Status` gives the rung, round trips, queue delay, loss and capacity for metrics.

## Control lease and freshness (`control`)

`LeaseState<T>` is the vehicle's control state for one session (`Epoch`, the vehicle's start time); `sat-teleop-control` drives it with milliseconds of the vehicle's monotonic clock and never with a ground timestamp.

- Only the `PeerId`s given to `LeaseState::new` may request, release or send (`NotController`, checked first; `controller` lets the caller refuse a stranger before reading anything). One lease at a time (`LeaseId`, holder `PeerId`, expiry). `request` grants a new lease unless another peer holds an unexpired one (`LeaseHeld`); the holder asking again gets a new lease and loses the old one. `release` ends it for its holder only. The lease lasts `lease_ms` after its grant or its holder's last admitted envelope.
- `issue_challenge` returns a `ChallengeToken` (a per-session counter: tokens prove freshness, not identity) only while a lease is unexpired; a new grant forgets earlier challenges.
- `admit` checks, in order: the epoch (`WrongEpoch`), the lease (`NoLease`, `NotHolder`, `WrongLease`, `LeaseExpired`), the challenge (one of the 4 most recent, `UnknownChallenge`; at most `challenge_ms` old, `ChallengeExpired`) the sequence number (above the last one of this lease and the envelope's `Kind`: liveness, continuous and discrete count separately, so a setpoint that overtakes a stop command cannot get it refused; `DuplicateSeq`, `ReorderedSeq`), and the transaction (below). A refused envelope changes nothing; an admitted one renews the lease and returns `Admitted`, which applying it requires.
- `ContinuousSlot` keeps only the newest setpoint. It is fresh until `setpoint_ms` after it arrived or until its challenge expires, whichever comes first, and only while the lease is; `setpoint` returns it only then, so refreshing stops on its own.
- A discrete envelope's `Header` carries its `Txn` (id and a digest of its command). `TxnCache` remembers the last 64 transactions applied per lease with their digests, least recently used dropped first. `admit` refuses a known id with another digest (`TxnConflict`, before changing anything) and marks a known id with the same digest `Admitted::repeated`: the caller answers it without applying it again. `record` remembers a transaction after it was applied (or used again).

## Regions (`region`)

`NormRect` is a rectangle in fractions of the picture, so a region means the same part of the picture at any rung. `NormRect::new` normalises corner order, clamps to the picture and returns `None` for a non-finite corner or a side under 1 %: a degenerate box is ignored, never an error. `pixels` rounds outwards, so the pixels always cover the box.

`active(pilot, detections, now_ms, limits)` gives the regions to keep sharp: the pilot's box first while at most `pilot_ttl_ms` old, then detections at most `detection_ttl_ms` old, largest first, `max_regions` in all; an empty list when their summed area exceeds `max_area_percent`. `RegionCell` hands the pilot's box from the vehicle's control loop to the sender; `sat-teleop-media` and `sat-teleop-control` share it through this crate, and with it a monotonic clock (`now_ms`) that `Timed::at_ms` counts on.

`RegionCell` also holds the follow of the pilot's box, once `enable_follow` has been called: `set` with a box other than the one followed starts it (the ground repeats the same box every 500 ms, and a repeat, or the same box after a follow was lost, starts nothing; `clear` forgets it), `follow_order` is what the tracker is to follow, `follow_moved` and `follow_lost` are its answers (ignored when they name an older box), and `followed` is where the target is now if the tracker said so within `detection_ttl_ms`. With the follow on, `regions` keeps sharp and `confirmed` tells the ground the followed box, never the box that was drawn, and a lost follow leaves neither.

## Following (`follow`)

`Follower::step(frame, order, detections)` takes one `WIDTH` x `HEIGHT` (320 x 180) luma picture, whatever the camera's shape, and returns `Idle`, `Moved(rect, score)`, `Held(score)` or `Lost(reason)`. A new `Order::generation` starts the follow: the seed snaps to the detection it overlaps by more than half (intersection over union) and its pixels become the template. Every later frame finds the template again by normalised cross-correlation within `SEARCH_PX` of the last place, over windows that lie inside the picture; a brightness or contrast change leaves the score alone. A match of 0.7 or better moves the template one part in eight towards what matched, so a target that slowly changes its look keeps its score; the template never learns from a worse match, so a target that leaves or is hidden does not teach it the background. Measured on the simulated people (`docs/measurement.md`, "Following"): a fixed template fell from 1.0 to 0.73 over a yaw and under 0.6 a few seconds later in the run that lost its person, where this holds 0.8 to 0.9. `Held` is a score under `Settings::min_score`: the box is not reported as moved, and `lost_frames` of them in a row end the follow as `Lost::Score`. A match that reaches an edge of the picture the box did not touch at the start ends it as `Lost::Edge`; a seed smaller than 8 px or flatter than a standard deviation of 4 grey levels is `Lost::Flat` at once. A template over 3000 samples is sampled on a lattice, so a building costs what a person does. No allocation in the search; every pixel read is a checked slice.

## Softening (`soften`)

`soften_i420` replaces every `factor` x `factor` luma block (`factor / 2`, at least 1, on chroma) outside the active regions with its block average, on the rung-sized frame before it is encoded; blocks start at the plane's origin, a partial edge block averages what it has, and kept blocks are untouched byte for byte. `factor` 1 or no region changes nothing. `outline` draws a box border clipped to the plane. Both run on every frame on a Pi: no allocation in the pixel loops, no indexing of input-sized buffers, padding bytes beyond the width are never written.

## Retention (`retention`)

`evict(objects, limits, free_bytes, now_unix_ms, now) -> Vec<usize>` says which retained snapshots to delete, oldest first by capture time: every one older than `max_age_ms`, then the oldest until the store holds at most `max_bytes` and the volume at least `min_free_bytes` (each eviction frees its bytes). A snapshot with `pinned_until` after `now` is skipped (a retrieval is reading it), so a store whose limits only pinned objects could meet stays over them until the pins expire. Capture times are wall clock (they name the file); pins are `Instant`s. An `Object` with `outage` set (taken while the ground was not heard, not fetched since) is skipped like a pinned one while it is within `outage_keep_ms` of its capture and within the outage share: the newest such objects first, until the next would take them past `outage_share_percent` of `max_bytes`, so a full share drops the oldest outage objects to ordinary ones. They outlive `max_age_ms`. A fetched object is no longer marked (`outage` false) and is ordinary.

## Position (`position`)

`Position` is the autopilot's latitude and longitude (degrees times 1e7), altitude above home in millimetres and heading in centidegrees (`None` when unknown). `fresh(newest, now)` returns the newest only when it was received at most `MAX_AGE` (2 s) before `now`; it reads no clock.

## Retrieval (`retrieval`)

Retrieval of retained detail yields to live video and control. `permit(&Conditions) -> Result<(), Hold>` allows the next chunk only with a non-zero allowance, at a `Normal` rung, not draining, with the relay's feedback fresh (an answer within `stale_after_ms`) and showing no queue (queue delay at most `queue_delay_base_ms`): a queue is the first sign retrieval is costing the video, so it stops itself on one, and unknown feedback is no permission. `ground_heard(&Conditions)` is the same freshness test as a boolean: the vehicle marks a snapshot taken while it is false as an outage snapshot. `Feedback::retrieval` is what retrieval reserves while a job runs, on the wire: `Controller` takes it out of the measured uplink with `Budget::split` before it fits the video to it at a cut. `chunk_size(allowance)` is what the allowance carries in half a second within 512 B to 16 KiB, so a chunk fits the one-second token bucket; `request_wire_bytes(payload)` is a conservative wire cost of one HTTP request (headers, TCP segments in `WireGuard`, the acknowledgement).

## Checks

```sh
cargo test -p sat-teleop-policy --locked
```

`landing` decides whether a sighting of the landing marker becomes a `LANDING_TARGET` and with which angles: `decide` (one pure function, one table test) withholds a frame older than `SIGHTING_MAX_AGE`, a camera more than `MAX_OFF_NADIR_DEG` from straight down (`Camera::Fixed { tilt_deg }`, or a gimbal `Mount` at most `MOUNT_MAX_GAP` from the sighting, before or after), a picture whose top is more than `MAX_TURN_DEG` from the nose (`Withheld::Turned`) and a missing height; `off_nadir_deg` and `picture_turn_deg` turn the mount's quaternion (roll and pitch relative to the horizon, yaw relative to the airframe) into those two angles. `Sighting.at` is when the frame left the tee, not its capture. Nothing in it reads a clock or the autopilot.

`kind` is the detector's vocabulary: `Kind` (person, bicycle, car, motorcycle, bus, truck, boat) with its COCO class, config name, tag letter (P, B, C, M, U for bus, T, S for boat), outline colour in YUV (chosen to differ in luma as well as hue, since a low bitrate keeps luma better; people stay amber) and 5x7 glyph, and `soften::tag` draws the letter in a solid tab inside a box's top-left corner (nothing when the tab does not fit). `sighting` keeps what the detector saw out of reach, each with its kind: `Sightings::record` merges a detection into the sighting it repeats (of the same kind, later than its last, within `MERGE_WITHIN_MS` and `MERGE_DISTANCE` of its box centre) or adds one, dropping the oldest past `MAX_SIGHTINGS` (256); `send_next` offers the oldest unsent one to a sender and marks it sent only if the sender says it went; `status_text` words one as a `STATUSTEXT` starting with the kind, of at most 50 characters (`motorcycle` is the longest) (positions rounded half away from zero to five places). Its table tests cover the merge rule, the bound, the single send and the text's rounding and length.

`retention` has two table tests: the limits (within every limit, over the quota, too old, below the free-space floor, input order, a pinned object skipped, a pin that ran out, everything pinned) and outage objects (kept past the maximum age, ordinary ones go first over the quota, the newest win a full share, fetched and over-age ones are ordinary). `position` has one: fresh for 2 s, not after, never. `budget` has one table test: the shares never add up to more than the total, control first (the larger of reserve and measurement), telemetry its share of what is offered within floor and cap, at totals from zero up, and the telemetry cap never below the floor. `adapt` has one table test over scripted links (a probe every 250 ms, each answer arriving one round trip later), one row per rule: steady noisy feedback holds the top, recovery waits out the dwell, a rising queue cuts at once to the rung the measured uplink carries, with headroom, a slowly draining queue is no base shift, a draining queue is not cut again, the capacity measured at a cut holds the climb, a standing queue is cut one rung, a step in the base delay is taken back, a drain ends when the delay falls too slowly for a queue, the loss bands, one change per interval, an outage falls to the floor and comes back to the rung before it, no higher than the capacity measured carries, a failed climb doubles its rung's hold from that cut and stays paced however old the measurement, repeated outages on a link that cannot carry the pre-outage rung overshoot once, silence after a deep queue is no outage, no answer yet holds the start rung, silence during a drain resumes the video, a failed climb doubles its rung's hold, a little more uplink does not give it back (cut and drained within the change interval; the capacity, kept through a blackout, still holds the climb after it), and a deep queue drains with the video paused, a drain measures the uplink again and resumes at the rung it carries. Rows on a `Queue` link simulate a FIFO uplink fed by the rung the controller picks. `pace` has one table test (a frame joins the backlog while what is ahead of it leaves within the age limit, with all its packets; after a drop delta frames are dropped until a keyframe fits, and only the first drop asks for a keyframe). `control` has one table test: a session timeline in which each step's result pins one rule (one lease for two controllers, wrong epoch, expired and unknown challenges, duplicate and reordered sequence numbers, a retried transaction not run again, setpoint expiry, lease expiry, release and reacquisition). `freshness` has one table test over its transitions (a frozen source over a healthy transport, unreadable stamps, a missing or too wide clock bound, a blackout before and after the disconnect threshold, two and one frames a second steady at LowRate on either side of a frame, a low rate past its own threshold, one frame a second repeating itself, a single gap at full rate then silence, full rate as old), each row also pinning when the state may next change; `clock` has two table tests (a bound from chrony's tracking line, none when unsynchronised or malformed; two bounds adding up only while both are fresh) and `token` one (a request is served only with the token, or from loopback when none is configured). `predict` has one table test for the aim (rate times lead, clamping, a stale attitude), one for the horizon (level, pitch, roll, a camera tilted down, off picture) and one for when a mark is drawn (a gimballed camera gets no horizon); `mark` tests that the ring is dashed, hollow and clear of the stamp rows. `stamp` round-trips the state stamp (every state) below the stamp and refuses a row with a corrupted CRC.

Tests are pure and table-driven; keep new ones to behaviour (round trip, rejection, wrap-around).
